## Overview

Route: London, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Belfast, Dublin.

An eight-day city-hopping route across the UK and Ireland, beginning with a direct flight from Delhi to London. The longest travel legs happen overnight or in the morning, leaving the day free for sightseeing.

<div class="callout">

### Plan these ahead

- **Edinburgh Castle.** Book a timed ticket in advance, especially for summer; allow at least two hours inside. [Check tickets](https://www.edinburghcastle.scot/plan-your-visit/tickets/).
- **Titanic Belfast.** Make this the first fixed booking in Belfast, then build the rest of the day around it. [Check experiences](https://titanicbelfast.org/experiences.html).
- **Glasgow to Belfast.** The usual rail-and-sail routing goes via Cairnryan; book the coach/ferry combination as one journey where possible. The crossing itself is about 2 hours 15 minutes. [Check the ferry](https://www.stenaline.co.uk/routes/cairnryan-belfast).
- **Belfast to Dublin.** The Enterprise runs from Belfast Grand Central to Dublin Connolly. Check the current timetable and reserve early for the departure you want. [Check services](https://www.translink.co.uk/using-translink/cross-border-travel/enterprise-train-service).

</div>

### Pace and routing

London and Dublin are proper walking days. In Edinburgh, do the castle first, walk downhill through the Old Town, then save the hill viewpoints for later if the weather is clear. Glasgow is mainly an overnight stop before the sea crossing, so use it for a compact city-centre walk rather than trying to cover the whole city.

Carry your passport and any visa documentation through the UK–Ireland transfer. Timetables, ticket rules and attraction opening hours change, so confirm every booking for your travel dates.

## Day 1, Delhi to London

Fly from Delhi to London. This is a recovery day, so stay central enough that the next morning's Westminster route starts without a long commute.

- Check in, buy an eSIM or sort data, and make sure the payment cards you plan to use work for contactless transport.
- Take a short orientation walk around your neighbourhood rather than stacking in an attraction after a long-haul flight.
- Confirm the next day's timed tickets, if you plan to enter Westminster Abbey or ride the London Eye. Both are easy to see from outside, but the experience is much better with a fixed slot.
- Early night. Day 2 covers a lot of pavement, and the route is best started fresh.

## Day 2, London

Walking day through central London. Start at Trafalgar Square and work south and west, so the major landmarks fall into a clean, mostly one-directional route. Westminster is a dense cluster: Parliament, Big Ben and Westminster Abbey sit close together, while Buckingham Palace and Hyde Park make a natural second half.

- **Morning — Trafalgar Square to the river.** Begin at Trafalgar Square, then walk down Whitehall. Take in the government buildings and Churchill War Rooms area on the way to Parliament Square. Big Ben is the bell; the tower itself is Elizabeth Tower, on the edge of the Palace of Westminster.
- **Late morning — Westminster.** Photograph Big Ben and the Houses of Parliament from Parliament Square or across Westminster Bridge. Visit Westminster Abbey if it is a priority; otherwise continue along the river. [Parliament tours](https://www.parliament.uk/visiting/) are separate from simply seeing the exterior.
- **Afternoon — London Eye and South Bank.** Cross Westminster Bridge to the London Eye. Ride it only if the view matters to you; the South Bank walk works well either way. Leave time for the riverside views back towards Parliament.
- **Late afternoon — Buckingham Palace.** Return across the river or through St James's Park for the walk to Buckingham Palace. If you want to see Changing the Guard, check the official schedule rather than assuming it runs that day. [Read about the ceremony](https://www.royal.uk/changing-the-guard?page=3).
- **Evening — Hyde Park.** Walk through Green Park and Hyde Park as the day winds down. It is a calmer finish than trying to add another paid attraction. Eat near Soho, Covent Garden or your hotel after the park.

## Day 3, London

Keep this as a flexible London day before the overnight bus to Edinburgh. Use it for the attractions that did not fit on the Westminster walk, but avoid a full-day excursion—bags, check-out and the late departure make this a deliberately loose day.

- **Museum option:** British Museum for its collection, Tate Modern for modern art and a river walk, or the National Gallery right back at Trafalgar Square. Pick one rather than rushing through several.
- **Neighbourhood option:** wander Covent Garden, Soho and Chinatown for food, then use the afternoon for shopping or a cafe break close to your coach departure point.
- **London practicals:** collect luggage before heading to the coach terminal, bring a warm layer for the overnight bus, keep valuables and travel documents with you, and download tickets/maps while you have good data.
- **Overnight:** take the night bus to Edinburgh. An overnight journey saves a hotel night but means Day 4 needs a realistic pace—use luggage storage or your accommodation before sightseeing.

## Day 4, Edinburgh to Glasgow

Arrive in Edinburgh early morning and explore before continuing to Glasgow in the evening. This is ambitious but workable if you pick one major paid attraction and let the rest be outdoors. The central sights are walkable, although Arthur's Seat is a genuine hike rather than a quick viewpoint.

- **First stop — Edinburgh Castle.** Go straight here after dropping your bags. The castle opens at 9:30am and the official guidance recommends allowing at least two hours; pre-book in summer. Focus on the Royal Palace, Honours of Scotland, Great Hall, Mons Meg and the city views. If your timing aligns, the One o'Clock Gun fires daily except Sundays, Good Friday and Christmas Day.
- **Late morning — Royal Mile.** Walk downhill from the castle along the Old Town's historic spine. Dip into a close or two, pause at St Giles' Cathedral, and continue towards the Palace of Holyroodhouse. Do not over-plan this stretch: its lanes and shopfronts are the point.
- **Afternoon — choose your route.** For a low-effort detour, walk through Princes Street Gardens to Dean Village, a photogenic former milling area by the Water of Leith. For the skyline, choose **either** Arthur's Seat or Calton Hill. Arthur's Seat needs sturdy shoes, time and dry-enough conditions; Calton Hill is much faster and easier before a train.
- **Whisky experience.** Fit this in near the castle/Royal Mile if you want a guided tasting. Book a time that still leaves a buffer for retrieving luggage and reaching Waverley.
- **Evening — Edinburgh to Glasgow.** Head to Glasgow after dinner. Keep the transfer simple: Waverley is central, and the journey is short enough that there is no need to leave Edinburgh unusually early.

## Day 5, Glasgow to Belfast

Travel from Glasgow to Belfast in the morning. This is primarily a transport day: the Scotland–Northern Ireland crossing normally involves a coach or rail connection to Cairnryan, then the ferry to Belfast. Build in the terminal check-in time rather than treating the sailing time as the whole journey.

- **If you have time before departure:** take a compact Glasgow city-centre loop around George Square, the City Chambers and Merchant City. Glasgow's centre is easy to cover on foot and is particularly good for Victorian architecture, street art, cafés and a quick meal.
- **To the ferry:** travel from Glasgow to Cairnryan, then sail to Belfast. Stena Line's crossing is around 2 hours 15 minutes; booking a combined Rail & Sail or coach-and-ferry itinerary reduces the chance of a missed connection.
- **Arrival in Belfast:** check in, then walk the central core around City Hall and the Cathedral Quarter if you still have energy. Keep the heavier Titanic visit for the next day, when you can give it enough time.
- **Packing note:** keep travel documents, medication, a charger and a warm layer in your day bag—the day is split between coach, terminal and ferry rather than one straightforward train ride.

## Day 6, Belfast to Dublin

Start in Belfast, then take the roughly two-hour journey to Dublin. Make Titanic Belfast the main event and decide whether Carrickfergus Castle is worth the time; doing both before a Dublin transfer makes the day tight.

- **Morning — Titanic Belfast.** Start at opening time in Titanic Quarter. The museum stands beside the slipways, drawing offices and graving dock where the ship was designed, built and launched. Allow several hours for the exhibitions rather than treating it as a photo stop.
- **Add SS Nomadic if time allows.** The restored White Star Line tender sits next to Titanic Belfast and makes a natural extension for anyone interested in the maritime story.
- **Optional — Carrickfergus Castle.** This is a separate trip east of Belfast on Belfast Lough. Choose it only if your train to Dublin is later and you are happy to skip a slower Belfast lunch or extra time at Titanic Quarter.
- **Belfast to Dublin.** Take the Enterprise from Belfast Grand Central to Dublin Connolly. The current journey is a little over two hours, but pick the exact service before committing to the museum timing. Arriving at Connolly puts you within easy reach of central Dublin.
- **Dublin evening:** check in, have dinner around the city centre, and save the main sights for Day 7. Temple Bar is liveliest after dark, but it is more enjoyable as a short stop than the whole night.

## Day 7, Dublin

Walking day through central Dublin. The route works best as a south-to-north loop: begin in the Georgian quarter by St Stephen's Green, loop through Dublin Castle and St Patrick's Cathedral, then finish by the River Liffey around Temple Bar and the Ha'penny Bridge.

- **Morning — St Stephen's Green.** Begin in the park for a quiet start, then walk along Grafton Street if you want shopping, buskers and coffee before the historic sites.
- **Late morning — Dublin Castle.** Take a guided tour if you want the State Apartments, medieval undercroft and Chapel Royal; the castle grounds alone make a useful short stop if you prefer to keep the day outdoors.
- **Early afternoon — St Patrick's Cathedral.** Walk south to the cathedral, then pause in the adjoining park. Give the interior enough time if you are interested in its history; otherwise it works well as an exterior stop on the route.
- **Late afternoon — Temple Bar and the Liffey.** Head north through Temple Bar's cobbled lanes, then cross or photograph the Ha'penny Bridge. The area is compact and crowded, so use it for atmosphere and a drink rather than trying to tick off everything quickly.
- **Evening:** return to whichever area you liked most—Grafton Street for a polished restaurant/bar scene, or Temple Bar for music and the buzz. Have your airport transfer and departure time ready tonight, so the final morning is calm.

## Day 8, Dublin to Delhi

Head to the airport for the return flight. Leave extra time for the airport transfer, bag drop and security; keep your passport, boarding pass and any tax-refund paperwork in the bag you carry, not in checked luggage.

If your flight is later in the day, use the final few hours for coffee and a short centre walk rather than starting another major attraction. Dublin Castle, St Stephen's Green and the Liffey are all close enough for one last loop if you are staying centrally.
