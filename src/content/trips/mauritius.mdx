## Overview

Route: La Gaulette, Grand Baie, Port Louis, east coast, airport belt.

A self-drive loop around the island, anchored on two apartment stays and a final night near the airport. Fly into Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport (MRU), pick up a rental car, and work clockwise: southwest first for **Grand Bassin**, **Black River Gorges National Park**, **Chamarel**, and **Le Morne**; then north via **Curepipe** and **Pamplemousses** to **Grand Baie** for the island-tour and beach stretch; then **Port Louis**; then east for the lagoon stops and the final **five-island tour** before an early morning departure.

May through December is the drier window on the west and north coasts, which lines up well with this route.

### Getting around

A rental car for the full trip is the move. Pick it up at the airport after you land and freshen up at the airport; I left around 10 AM once the car was ready. Drop it at the airport on departure morning. The main roads are fine for a compact car; island-hopping days are organised tours that handle the boat legs.

For the island tours, it is worth contacting multiple operators before you book; pricing, pickup points, and inclusions vary more than you would expect. I booked through **MTB TRAVEL AND TOURS LTD** and the experience was great.

### SIM and money

The local currency is the **Mauritian rupee (MUR)**. I took US dollars from India and exchanged at the airport on arrival. Euro is also widely used on the island if you would rather carry euros.

A local SIM from the airport on arrival is the easy option if you want data for maps and tour confirmations on the road. Immigration is straightforward for Indian passport holders, but you do need to fill the Mauritius digital travel form before arrival; see the [visa notes](/wanderlust/visa#mauritius).

### Vegetarian food

Vegetarian food is straightforward across the island. Indian restaurants are common, and most places will do veg curries, roti, and rice plates if you ask.

## Day 1, Arrival and the southwest

Land at Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport and freshen up at the airport before the rental pickup. I left around 10 AM once the car was ready.

Breakfast on the drive south, then **Grand Bassin** (Ganga Talao), the sacred crater lake in an old volcanic crater and one of the island's main Hindu pilgrimage sites. The giant **Mangal Mahadev** Shiva statue at the entrance is the big landmark here, and the temple complex around the lake is an easy first stop after the flight.

**Black River Gorges National Park** filled the rest of the afternoon, the island's biggest protected forest and the best first look at interior Mauritius. Expect viewpoints over deep green valleys, short walks if you do not want a full hike, and the chance of spotting some of the island's endemic birdlife. Check in at La Gaulette for the southwest stretch.

## Day 2, Chamarel

Day trip to **Chamarel**: the Seven Coloured Earths, the nearby waterfall, and the rum distilleries in the area. The coloured dunes are one of Mauritius' geological oddities, volcanic sand that settles into visible bands of different shades, and the viewpoints are quick but very worth it.

The Seven Coloured Earths site is private property, so you need a ticket for entry; you can buy it online or at the entrance, and both cash and card are accepted. Pair that with **Chamarel Falls**, the island's highest single-drop waterfall, and a distillery stop if that interests you.

In the evening, head to **Le Morne**. It is one of the island's signature coastal landscapes, with the mountain rising straight behind the lagoon, and works well as a slower sunset-side finish to the day. The winding drive itself is part of the appeal.

## Day 3, Northbound via Curepipe and Pamplemousses

Checkout from La Gaulette and drive north. Break the drive with **Trou aux Cerfs** in Curepipe, the island's dormant volcanic crater and one of the easiest central plateau viewpoints to fit into a road day.

It sits about 605 metres above sea level, with a circular road around the crater and wide views over Curepipe and the surrounding uplands when the weather is clear. Quick stop, easy access, and a good reminder that Mauritius is volcanic at its core, not just beaches.

Continue north via **Pamplemousses**, best known for the SSR Botanical Garden area and the old estate belt around it, before checking in at Grand Baie.

## Day 4, Northern islands

Daytime northern-islands tour to **Flat Island**, **Gabriel Island**, and **Coin de Mire**. **Coin de Mire** is the dramatic cliff-backed reserve you cruise past, while the swimming, beach time, and snorkelling are usually around Flat Island and Gabriel Island, where the water turns that bright north-coast blue Mauritius is known for.

## Day 5, Cap Malheureux and north-coast beach time

North-coast day around **Notre-Dame Auxiliatrice** at **Cap Malheureux** and **Mont Choisy Beach**. The red-roofed church at Cap Malheureux is one of the island's classic photo stops, set right on the water with views out toward the northern islets.

Pair that with time at **Mont Choisy Beach**, one of the long public beaches in the north, good for a slower swim-and-walk day without overplanning it.

## Day 6, Port Louis and Grand Baie

Day trip to **Port Louis** for the urban side of Mauritius: the **Central Market**, **Caudan Waterfront**, the **Citadel** (Fort Adelaide), and **Aapravasi Ghat**. The market is the busy local stop for snacks, fruit, and souvenirs; the waterfront is the cleaner promenade version of the city; the citadel gives the best elevated view back over the harbour.

**Aapravasi Ghat** is the historical stop here, the UNESCO-listed immigration depot tied to the island's indentured-labour history. Back to **Grand Baie** after for street food in the evening.

## Day 7, East coast and airport hotel

Checkout from Grand Baie and head east. Then the coastal stops around **Mahébourg**, **Pointe d'Esny**, and **Blue Bay** for lagoon views and beach time. Mahébourg is one of the older towns on the island, Pointe d'Esny is the calmer postcard stop, and **Blue Bay Marine Park** is the well-known snorkelling lagoon on this side.

Check in at the hotel, near **Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport**, a great location for the early flight out. The hotel I stayed at ran complimentary shuttles to beaches, which is useful if you want one last easy coastal outing without driving again.

## Day 8, Five-island tour

Full-day **five-island tour** on the east coast, usually by speedboat out of **Trou d'Eau Douce** or **Blue Bay**. The classic circuit covers **Ile aux Cerfs**, **Ile aux Phares** (Lighthouse Island), **Ile de la Passe**, **Ile aux Aigrettes**, and a stop at the **Grand River South East waterfall**.

**Ile aux Aigrettes** is the conservation stop, a protected islet preserving the last dry coastal forest and known for giant tortoises and rare endemic species; **Ile aux Cerfs** is the opposite mood, all lagoon water, sandbars, and classic east-coast beach time.

Another pre-book day, and again, it is worth checking with multiple operators before you commit so you can compare boat size, lunch, snorkelling stops, and timing.

## Day 9, Departure

Early start to the airport, drop the rental car, and fly home.
