## Overview

Route: Kathmandu, Pokhara, Kathmandu.

A short Kathmandu and Pokhara split: land in Kathmandu, ease in with Thamel and the old-city sights, then fly across to Pokhara for the lakeside stretch and the sunrise outing to Sarangkot, before circling back to Kathmandu for the temples and the flight home.

Nepal's broad sweet spots are **spring (March to May)** and **autumn (September to November)**, when skies are clearest and mountain views are most reliable. This trip was in January, which still worked fine for a city-and-Pokhara route, just colder in the mornings and evenings.

### Flights

The Kathmandu to Pokhara flight is the time-saver on this route. It is a short domestic hop, around 20 minutes in the air, and cuts out the long road transfer.

### SIM and money

The local currency is the **Nepalese rupee (NPR)**.

**INR 100 notes** were accepted at many places. I exchanged some Nepalese rupees at shops once I got in, and kept cash for the smaller spends.

Either get an **eSIM** before you fly, or pick up the SIM at the airport itself and get the **Pathao** app set up so you can book a cab from the airport.

Otherwise you are stuck either booking the prepaid airport taxi, which is more expensive, or bargaining on the road after you step out.

## Day 1, Arrival in Kathmandu

Fly **DEL to KTM**, check in, then keep the first day easy.

**Thamel** is the soft landing zone for this trip: dense with cafes, gear shops, money changers, bars, and tour counters, so it is the easiest part of Kathmandu to settle into on arrival and sort small logistics.

- Explore **Thamel** on foot
- Book or confirm the next day's city plan
- Dinner and relax after the flight

If you land with some daylight left, just use it for a slow wander. Kathmandu can feel noisy and chaotic on first contact, so the better move on day one is to keep the radius tight instead of trying to cram in monuments straight away.

## Day 2, Kathmandu sightseeing

Classic Kathmandu heritage day.

Start with **Patan Durbar Square**, one of the three royal squares of the valley and part of the wider **Kathmandu Valley UNESCO World Heritage Site**. This is the place for carved Newar architecture, temple courtyards, old palace buildings, and a slower, more atmospheric old-city feel than central Kathmandu.

- **Patan Durbar Square**
- **Swayambhunath** (Monkey Temple)
- More Kathmandu sightseeing around the old city

Then head to **Swayambhunath**, the hilltop stupa west of the city. It is one of Nepal's most important Buddhist sites, also part of the UNESCO listing, and it gives you both the religious complex and the city view in one stop. The monkey nickname is real, so keep your food and sunglasses in hand.

This works best as a two-anchor day rather than a checklist day. Patan is worth lingering in for the brick courtyards and temple details; Swayambhunath is best once the light softens a bit and the valley opens up below you.

## Day 3, Pokhara

Take the **11 AM flight to Pokhara**, check in, and sort the next day's tour.

Pokhara is the cleaner, calmer half of the trip. The city sits on **Phewa Lake** with the Annapurna range beyond it, and the whole mood shifts the moment you get there.

- Explore the market
- Lakeside walks
- Sunset at **Phewa Lake**

Keep this day light. The whole point is to settle into the lakeside area, walk without an agenda, and save your energy for the early start next morning. Sunset by the lake is the obvious first-evening move, especially if the sky is clear enough for the mountain outline to show.

## Day 4, Pokhara sightseeing

Early start for **Sarangkot** for sunrise, then a full-day Pokhara sightseeing tour by bus.

**Sarangkot** is the headline sunrise viewpoint above Pokhara, around **1,600 metres** up, known for wide Himalayan views toward **Annapurna, Dhaulagiri, and Manaslu** on a clear morning, along with the lake and the city spread below. This is the day to start early for; if the weather is on your side, it pays off.

- **Gupteshwar Cave**
- **Devi's Fall**
- **Pumdikot Shiva Statue**
- **World Peace Pagoda**
- **Hanging Bridge**
- **Bat Cave**
- **Mahendra Cave**
- Temple stop on the tour

Of the Pokhara city stops, the best-known pair is **Devi's Fall** and **Gupteshwar Cave**, which sit opposite each other. The cave is a major attraction in its own right, and the water from Devi's Fall passes through it. **World Peace Pagoda** is the big viewpoint stop on the south side of the lake, while **Pumdikot** gives you the giant Shiva statue and another elevated view over Pokhara.

This is usually a packed vehicle day rather than a slow wandering day. Good for covering the postcard sights in one shot; not the day for a long lunch or loose pacing.

## Day 5, Kathmandu

Fly or return back to Kathmandu and use the day for the other big temple-and-stupa circuit.

This is the more spiritual Kathmandu day, and it works well because the three major stops are distinct from each other in mood.

- **Pashupatinath**
- **Boudhanath Stupa**
- **Kathmandu Durbar Square**

**Pashupatinath** is one of the most important Shiva temples in South Asia, spread across a large sacred precinct on the **Bagmati River** and included in the valley's UNESCO listing. **Boudhanath** is one of the largest spherical stupas in Nepal and the centre of the Tibetan Buddhist quarter, best experienced by walking the clockwise kora around it instead of treating it as just a photo stop.

Finish with **Kathmandu Durbar Square**, the old royal centre at Hanuman Dhoka, another UNESCO-listed square with palace buildings and layered Newar temple architecture. If you still have energy in the evening, Boudha is the nicest place to linger after dark; Kathmandu Durbar is more of a daytime heritage stop.

## Day 6, Travel out

Early morning flight **KTM to DEL**.
