## Overview

Route: Mahe, Praslin (with a La Digue day trip), Mahe.

Land on **Mahe**, then use the inter-island ferries to base on **Praslin** for the middle stretch: a full day on **La Digue**, optional island tours and snorkel stops, before returning to Mahe for lagoon beach time and a final half-day in **Victoria** before the flight home.

The dry season on these islands runs roughly **April through October**, which lines up well with a September run like this one.

On **Mahe**, **east-coast beaches** are often **choppy**; for swimming and sunsets, look to the **south coast** or the **west** side of the island instead.

### Getting there

**Direct flights from Mumbai** to Seychelles (Mahe) are the straightforward option from India. From most other Indian cities you will usually connect **via Mumbai** with a single layover.

### Getting around

Inter-island travel between the three main islands (**Mahe**, **Praslin**, and **La Digue**) is by **ferry**. **Cat Cocos** is the official operator on these routes. **Book online in advance**; do not rely on last-minute tickets, especially when you are changing islands or doing a timed day trip.

**Mahe:** the ferry terminal is in **Victoria**. Search maps for **Cat Cocos Berth** so you know exactly where to board.

**Praslin:** use **Praslin Ferry Terminal** / **Praslin Ferry Harbour** on maps.

Book ahead in peak season and aim for morning sailings when you are changing bases; the **9 AM La Digue ferry** and a **5 PM return** work well for a day trip without an overnight bag.

There are also **short flights between Mahe and Praslin** if you want to skip the sea crossing or the schedule suits you better; price them against the ferry once your dates are fixed.

On each island, **taxis are very expensive**, so if you are staying multiple days on the same island, **rent a car** for that stretch rather than relying on point-to-point cabs. **Car rentals** and **island tour operators** mostly expect **cash** (SCR or euro) unless you book and pay online; confirm payment when you reserve.

On La Digue, **rent a bicycle at the jetty** (under INR 1,000 in our case). Pick **decent bikes** if you can: the island is not flat, and there are enough **ups and downs** that you will feel it if you are not especially fit (we struggled). The **southern part of the island is foot-only**, so map your loop in advance rather than assuming you can cycle everywhere.

**La Digue has its own accommodation**, so you can also **overnight there** and stretch or reshuffle this itinerary instead of doing a single day trip from Praslin.

### eSIM and money

The local currency is the **Seychellois rupee (SCR)**. **Euro and SCR** both circulate; confirm the currency and amount before you pay.

You can **exchange at the airport** on arrival. Cards tend to work in supermarkets and larger restaurants, but keep cash for ferries, rentals, smaller operators, and anything booked on the spot.

We had an **eSIM installed before landing**, so it activated as soon as we touched down in Seychelles, with no airport SIM queue.

### Vegetarian food

**Vegetarian food is scarce** on the islands compared with much of Asia or India. Eating out is **expensive** in general, so it helps to **carry ready-to-eat meals** from home for long days and late evenings.

**Takeaways** are often a bit cheaper than sit-down restaurants and are worth leaning on when you do not want a full restaurant bill.

## Day 1, Mahe arrival

Land on **Mahe**, check in, and keep the first afternoon easy after the flight.

- **Takamaka Rum Distillery** for a tour and tasting in the south of the island
- **Sunset at a south- or west-coast beach**; skip the east coast if the sea looks rough

Your **Cat Cocos** sailings for the rest of the trip should already be **booked online** before you land; if anything is still open, sort it now rather than gambling on walk-up availability.

## Day 2, Mahe to Praslin

Checkout, take the **Cat Cocos ferry to Praslin** from Victoria, check in, then head out for the island's headline stops.

- **Vallée de Mai**, a UNESCO-listed forest known for its coco de mer palms and endemic wildlife. Entry is paid, and we skipped it because we did not find it value for money.
- **Fond Ferdinand Nature Reserve** is a less expensive alternative that we also did not visit. At about 122 hectares, it is over six times larger than Vallée de Mai, with coco de mer palms, native wildlife and a viewpoint over Praslin; guided visits are available. [Source: Seychelles Travel Guide](https://www.seyvillas.com/en/guide/islands/praslin/naturreservat-fond-ferdinand); the Seychelles Parks and Gardens Authority also notes its lower entry fee in its [hiking guide](https://www.spga.gov.sc/sites/default/files/2022-03/hiking_in_seychelles.pdf).
- **Anse Georgette**, one of the north-coast coves on many Praslin lists. The access path runs through **Constance Lemuria** (the golf-course resort): you **park before the resort gate** and walk in, around **25 minutes** to the beach

If you choose one of the forest reserves, keep Anse Georgette for the late afternoon rather than rushing both.

## Day 3, La Digue day trip

Morning **9 AM ferry to La Digue**. Rent **good bicycles at the jetty** and loop the island at your own pace, with the **foot-only south** in mind when you plan the route.

- **Anse Source d'Argent**, the shallow, granite-backed bay that shows up on every Seychelles postcard
- We also stopped at **Anse Sévère**, **Anse Fourmis**, **Anse Grosse Roche** and **Anse Grand** along the way. Visiting these beaches by bicycle made for a relaxed loop around La Digue, with plenty of chances to pause and enjoy the coast between stops.
- **L'Union Estate** if you want the paid estate access tied to some of the classic photo angles (we skipped the paid entry, but it is easy to add)
- **Grand Anse** or **Petite Anse** on the windier east side for a different feel from the sheltered west-coast bays

Return on the **5 PM ferry** back to Praslin for the night.

*Alt: stay **overnight on La Digue** and give the island its own night or two as a base, instead of making it a day trip.*

## Day 4, Praslin

Choose between an organised water day or a slower beach day.

- **Curieuse island tour** with **snorkelling at St Pierre islet** is a solid full-day option if you want turtles, clear water, and a guided circuit
- *Alt:* relax on land and save your energy for **Anse Lazio**; snorkelling there works **early morning** or **evening** when the water is calmer and the light is good

We treated this as a flexible day: book Curieuse if the weather and schedule align, otherwise Anse Lazio is the fallback that never feels like a compromise.

## Day 5, Praslin to Mahe

Checkout, **ferry back to Mahe**, check in, then mix beach time with a few easy stops around the bridges off the main island.

- **Beau Vallon** for **snorkelling** off the main tourist beach strip on Mahe
- **Eden Island**, linked to Mahe by a **road bridge**. Much of the island is **residents-only**; visitors can see some of it, but a lot is closed to tourists
- **Perseverance Island**, also connected by a **road bridge**, for an outside look at the **Supreme Court** and **Parliament** buildings

Keep gear rinsed and ready; this is a good night to pack most of your bags before the Victoria morning.

## Day 6, Victoria

Spend the day exploring the capital at an easy pace.

- **Victoria**, the compact capital, on foot
- **Victoria market** for spices, fruit, and last-minute souvenirs
- **National Library** and the **museums** clustered in town if you want the indoor slice of the country before you fly
- A **beautiful small church on Mahe** we visited during the day

## Day 7, Mahe departure

Checkout and head to the airport for our flight home. The airport is small, but has a lounge, duty-free and a few other shops. The **Takamaka store** is a convenient place to pick up rum before flying; prices are among the cheapest we found, and buying at the airport means you do not have to carry bottles around during the trip. [Airport facilities](https://seychellesairports.sc/customer-centre) include a lounge, duty-free and souvenir shops.
